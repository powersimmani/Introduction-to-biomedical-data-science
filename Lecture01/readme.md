## Part 1: Atomic and Molecular Foundations

### 🔬 Atoms and Electron Orbitals

#### Electron Configuration
- Electrons occupy specific energy levels
- Quantum numbers define orbital characteristics
- Aufbau principle: fill lowest energy first
- Pauli exclusion principle

#### Valence Electrons
- Outermost shell electrons
- Determine chemical reactivity
- Participate in bond formation
- Critical for biological interactions

#### Orbital Shapes
- **s orbitals:** Spherical
- **p orbitals:** Dumbbell-shaped
- **d orbitals:** Complex shapes
- **f orbitals:** Very complex shapes

#### Biological Elements (CHNOPS)
The six essential elements that make up most biological molecules:
- **C** - Carbon
- **H** - Hydrogen
- **N** - Nitrogen
- **O** - Oxygen
- **P** - Phosphorus
- **S** - Sulfur

---

### ⚡ Chemical Bonds in Biology

#### Bond Energy Comparison (kcal/mol)
1. **Covalent Bonds:** 50-200 kcal/mol
   - Strong electron sharing
   - Single, double, triple bonds
   - Form backbone of biomolecules
   - Strongest bonds in biological systems

2. **Ionic Interactions:** 5-10 kcal/mol
   - Electrostatic attractions
   - Important in protein folding
   - Salt bridges stabilize structures
   - Moderate strength

3. **Hydrogen Bonds:** 1-5 kcal/mol
   - Weak but numerous
   - Critical for DNA base pairing
   - Protein secondary structure
   - Essential for molecular recognition

4. **Van der Waals Forces:** <1 kcal/mol
   - Weakest interactions
   - Important in close packing
   - Hydrophobic effect
   - Cumulative effects are significant

### 💧 Water - The Solvent of Life

#### Molecular Structure
- Bent geometry (104.5°)
- Polar covalent O-H bonds
- Partial charges: δ- O, δ+ H
- Strong dipole moment

#### Unique Properties
- High heat capacity
- High heat of vaporization
- Less dense as solid (ice floats)
- Excellent solvent for polar molecules

#### Hydrophobic Effect
- Nonpolar molecules cluster together
- Drives protein folding
- Forms lipid bilayers
- Entropy-driven process

#### Solvation
- Water molecules surround ions
- Hydration shells stabilize charges
- Affects biochemical reactions
- Critical for ion transport

#### H-Bond Network
- Water forms extensive hydrogen bond networks
- Dynamic and constantly reforming
- Critical for water's unique properties
- Enables life as we know it

### 🧪 pH and Biological Systems

#### pH Scale
- pH = -log[H⁺]
- Range from 0 (acidic) to 14 (basic)
- pH 7 is neutral
- Most biological systems operate at pH 6-8

#### Buffer Systems
- Resist changes in pH
- Bicarbonate buffer system in blood
- Phosphate buffer in cells
- Protein buffers

#### Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation
- pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])
- Describes pH of buffer solutions
- Predicts ionization states
- Essential for understanding biological pH

#### Enzyme pH Dependence
- Enzymes have optimal pH ranges
- pH affects enzyme structure and activity
- Ionization of active site residues
- Physiological pH regulation is critical

### 🧬 Amino Acids Structure

#### General Structure
- Central carbon (α-carbon)
- Amino group (-NH₂)
- Carboxyl group (-COOH)
- Side chain (R group)
- Hydrogen atom

#### Classification by Side Chain Properties
1. **Nonpolar/Hydrophobic:**
   - Glycine, Alanine, Valine, Leucine, Isoleucine
   - Methionine, Phenylalanine, Tryptophan, Proline

2. **Polar/Uncharged:**
   - Serine, Threonine, Cysteine, Tyrosine
   - Asparagine, Glutamine

3. **Positively Charged (Basic):**
   - Lysine, Arginine, Histidine

4. **Negatively Charged (Acidic):**
   - Aspartate, Glutamate

#### Chirality
- All amino acids (except glycine) are chiral
- L-form is found in proteins
- D-amino acids rare in nature
- Chirality affects protein structure

#### Ionization States
- Amino acids are zwitterions at physiological pH
- NH₃⁺ at N-terminus
- COO⁻ at C-terminus
- pH affects side chain ionization

### 🏗️ Protein Structure Levels

#### Primary Structure (1°)
- Linear sequence of amino acids
- Peptide bonds connect amino acids
- Determines all higher-level structures
- Encoded by DNA sequence

#### Secondary Structure (2°)
- **α-helix:**
  - Right-handed coil
  - 3.6 residues per turn
  - Stabilized by hydrogen bonds
  - Common in membrane proteins

- **β-sheet:**
  - Extended strands
  - Parallel or antiparallel
  - Hydrogen bonds between strands
  - Found in many structural proteins

- **Turns and loops:**
  - Connect secondary structures
  - Often on protein surface
  - Variable structures

#### Tertiary Structure (3°)
- Overall 3D shape of single polypeptide
- Hydrophobic core formation
- Disulfide bonds (cysteine bridges)
- Stabilized by multiple interactions

#### Quaternary Structure (4°)
- Multiple polypeptide chains
- Subunit arrangement
- Examples: Hemoglobin (4 subunits)
- Cooperative binding effects

### 🧬 Nucleotides and DNA Structure

#### Nucleotide Components
- Nitrogenous base (A, T, G, C)
- 5-carbon sugar (deoxyribose)
- Phosphate group
- Connected by glycosidic bond

#### Bases
- **Purines:** Adenine (A), Guanine (G)
  - Double-ring structure
  - Larger bases

- **Pyrimidines:** Thymine (T), Cytosine (C)
  - Single-ring structure
  - Smaller bases

- **Watson-Crick base pairing:**
  - A=T (2 hydrogen bonds)
  - G≡C (3 hydrogen bonds)

#### DNA Double Helix
- Antiparallel strands (5' to 3' and 3' to 5')
- Sugar-phosphate backbone
- Major and minor grooves
- Right-handed B-form (most common)

#### Structural Parameters
- Diameter: ~2 nm
- Rise per base pair: 0.34 nm
- 10.5 base pairs per turn
- Pitch: 3.57 nm

## Part 2: Central Dogma (DNA → RNA → Protein)

### 🔄 DNA Replication Mechanism

#### Semiconservative Replication
- Each strand serves as template
- Two identical daughter DNA molecules
- Proven by Meselson-Stahl experiment

#### Key Enzymes
1. **Helicase:** Unwinds DNA helix
2. **Primase:** Synthesizes RNA primers
3. **DNA Polymerase III:** Main replication enzyme (5'→3')
4. **DNA Polymerase I:** Removes primers, fills gaps
5. **Ligase:** Joins Okazaki fragments

#### Leading vs Lagging Strand
- **Leading strand:** Continuous synthesis (5'→3')
- **Lagging strand:** Discontinuous synthesis
- **Okazaki fragments:** 1000-2000 nucleotides
- Both synthesized simultaneously at replication fork

#### Proofreading & Fidelity
- 3'→5' exonuclease activity
- Error rate: ~1 in 10⁷ bases
- Mismatch repair systems
- Ensures genetic stability

---

### 📝 Transcription Process

#### Initiation
- RNA polymerase binds to promoter
- TATA box recognition (eukaryotes)
- Transcription factors required
- DNA unwinding begins

#### Elongation
- RNA polymerase moves along template strand
- Synthesis in 5'→3' direction
- RNA nucleotides added (A, U, G, C)
- Template strand read 3'→5'

#### Termination
- Specific termination sequences
- Rho-dependent or rho-independent
- RNA polymerase released
- DNA rewinds

#### RNA Processing (Eukaryotes)
- **5' capping:** 7-methylguanosine cap
- **3' polyadenylation:** Poly-A tail (~200 A's)
- **Splicing:** Removal of introns
- Alternative splicing increases diversity

### 🧬 Translation and Genetic Code

#### Ribosome Structure
- Large subunit (60S in eukaryotes)
- Small subunit (40S in eukaryotes)
- rRNA and ribosomal proteins
- Three tRNA sites: A (aminoacyl), P (peptidyl), E (exit)

#### tRNA Function
- Anticodon pairs with mRNA codon
- Carries specific amino acid
- Wobble base pairing at 3rd position
- Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases ensure accuracy

#### Translation Steps
1. **Initiation:**
   - Ribosome assembles at AUG start codon
   - First tRNA (Met) binds
   - Initiation factors required

2. **Elongation:**
   - tRNAs bring amino acids to A site
   - Peptide bond formation in P site
   - Ribosome translocates 3 nucleotides
   - Energy: 2 GTP per amino acid

3. **Termination:**
   - Stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA
   - Release factors bind
   - Polypeptide released
   - Ribosome disassembles

#### Genetic Code
- 64 codons total
- 61 code for amino acids
- 3 stop codons
- Degenerate code (multiple codons per amino acid)
- Universal with rare exceptions

### 🎛️ Gene Regulation Overview

#### Transcriptional Control
- Promoter strength
- Transcription factor binding
- RNA polymerase recruitment
- Most important regulatory level

#### Enhancers and Silencers
- **Enhancers:** Increase transcription
- **Silencers:** Decrease transcription
- Can work at long distances
- Looping brings them to promoter

#### Chromatin Remodeling
- Histone modifications affect accessibility
- ATP-dependent chromatin remodelers
- Open vs closed chromatin states
- Regulates gene availability

#### Post-transcriptional Regulation
- Alternative splicing
- mRNA stability
- microRNA regulation
- Translational control

---

### 🧬 Epigenetic Modifications

#### DNA Methylation
- Addition of methyl groups to cytosine
- Usually at CpG islands
- Generally silences genes
- Heritable through cell division

#### Histone Modifications
- **Acetylation:** Opens chromatin (active)
- **Methylation:** Can activate or repress
- **Phosphorylation:** Various effects
- **Ubiquitination:** Protein turnover signals

#### Chromatin States
- **Euchromatin:** Open, active transcription
- **Heterochromatin:** Closed, silent genes
- Dynamic and reversible
- Affects gene expression patterns

#### Disease Implications
- Cancer: Aberrant methylation patterns
- Imprinting disorders
- Environmental effects on epigenome
- Potential therapeutic targets

---

### 🧬 RNA Types and Functions

#### Messenger RNA (mRNA)
- Carries genetic information from DNA
- Template for protein synthesis
- 5' cap and 3' poly-A tail
- Contains coding sequence (CDS)

#### Transfer RNA (tRNA)
- Brings amino acids to ribosome
- Cloverleaf secondary structure
- Anticodon for codon recognition
- 75-90 nucleotides long

#### Ribosomal RNA (rRNA)
- Structural and catalytic component of ribosomes
- Most abundant RNA type
- Peptidyl transferase activity
- Highly conserved

#### Small Regulatory RNAs
- **miRNA:** Gene silencing (~22 nt)
- **siRNA:** RNA interference
- **snoRNA:** rRNA modification
- **snRNA:** Splicing machinery

#### Long Non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs)
- >200 nucleotides
- Regulatory functions
- Chromatin modification
- Scaffold for protein complexes

---

### 🧬 Protein Folding and Misfolding

#### Anfinsen's Principle
- Primary structure determines tertiary structure
- Protein folding is thermodynamically favorable
- Native state is lowest energy
- Demonstrated with RNase A

#### Chaperone Proteins
- **Heat Shock Proteins (HSPs):** Prevent aggregation
- **Chaperonins (GroEL/GroES):** Provide folding chamber
- **Protein Disulfide Isomerase:** Forms correct S-S bonds
- ATP-dependent mechanisms

#### Folding Funnels
- Energy landscape perspective
- Multiple pathways to native state
- Kinetic traps possible
- Molten globule intermediates

#### Misfolding Diseases
1. **Alzheimer's Disease:**
   - Amyloid-β plaques
   - Tau tangles
   - Neurotoxic aggregates

2. **Parkinson's Disease:**
   - α-synuclein aggregates
   - Lewy bodies
   - Dopaminergic neuron death

3. **Prion Diseases:**
   - PrPᶜ → PrPˢᶜ conversion
   - Self-propagating
   - Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease

4. **Cystic Fibrosis:**
   - CFTR misfolding
   - Protein degraded before reaching membrane
   - Loss of function

## Part 3: Cellular Systems and Integration

### 🧱 Cell Membrane Structure

#### Lipid Bilayer
- Phospholipids with hydrophobic tails
- Hydrophilic heads face aqueous environment
- Cholesterol modulates fluidity
- Asymmetric composition

#### Membrane Proteins
- **Integral proteins:** Span membrane
- **Peripheral proteins:** Surface-associated
- **Glycoproteins:** Carbohydrate modifications
- Functions: transport, signaling, adhesion

#### Fluid Mosaic Model
- Lipids and proteins move laterally
- Dynamic structure
- Lipid rafts concentrate proteins
- Temperature affects fluidity

#### Transport Mechanisms
- **Passive transport:** Diffusion, no energy
- **Facilitated diffusion:** Channel/carrier proteins
- **Active transport:** Against gradient, ATP required
- **Endocytosis/Exocytosis:** Vesicle-mediated

---

### 🏭 Organelles and Functions

#### Nucleus
- Contains genetic material
- Nuclear envelope with pores
- Nucleolus: rRNA synthesis
- Chromatin organization

#### Endoplasmic Reticulum
- **Rough ER:** Ribosomes, protein synthesis
- **Smooth ER:** Lipid synthesis, detoxification
- Continuous with nuclear envelope
- Protein folding and modification

#### Mitochondria
- ATP production via oxidative phosphorylation
- Own DNA (maternal inheritance)
- Double membrane
- Citric acid cycle occurs in matrix

#### Golgi Apparatus
- Protein modification and sorting
- Glycosylation
- Vesicle formation
- Cis to trans processing

#### Lysosomes
- Digestive enzymes
- Low pH environment
- Autophagy
- Cellular waste recycling

#### Peroxisomes
- Fatty acid oxidation
- H₂O₂ breakdown
- Detoxification
- Separate from mitochondria

### 📡 Cell Signaling Pathways

#### Receptor Types
1. **G-Protein Coupled Receptors (GPCRs):**
   - 7 transmembrane domains
   - G-protein activation
   - Most drug targets

2. **Receptor Tyrosine Kinases (RTKs):**
   - Growth factor receptors
   - Phosphorylation cascades
   - Cancer-related mutations

3. **Ion Channel Receptors:**
   - Ligand-gated channels
   - Fast synaptic transmission
   - Neuronal signaling

4. **Nuclear Receptors:**
   - Intracellular receptors
   - Gene transcription
   - Steroid hormones

#### Second Messengers
- **cAMP:** Activates PKA
- **Ca²⁺:** Multiple targets
- **IP₃/DAG:** From PIP₂ cleavage
- Amplify signals

#### Kinase Cascades
- MAPK pathway (Ras→Raf→MEK→ERK)
- Phosphorylation amplification
- Signal integration
- Regulation of gene expression

#### Feedback Regulation
- Negative feedback prevents overactivation
- Positive feedback amplifies signals
- Receptor desensitization
- Signal termination mechanisms

### ⚡ Metabolic Pathways Overview

#### Glycolysis
- Glucose → 2 Pyruvate
- Cytoplasmic pathway
- Net: 2 ATP, 2 NADH
- Anaerobic process

#### TCA Cycle (Krebs Cycle)
- Pyruvate → Acetyl-CoA → CO₂
- Mitochondrial matrix
- Produces: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP per turn
- Central metabolic hub

#### Oxidative Phosphorylation
- Electron transport chain
- Proton gradient generation
- ATP synthase produces ATP
- ~30-32 ATP per glucose

#### Pathway Integration
- Glycolysis ↔ Gluconeogenesis
- Fatty acid synthesis ↔ β-oxidation
- Amino acid metabolism
- Coordinated regulation

---

### 🔋 ATP and Energy Transfer

#### ATP Structure
- Adenine base
- Ribose sugar
- Three phosphate groups
- High-energy phosphate bonds

#### Energy Coupling
- ΔG of ATP hydrolysis: -30.5 kJ/mol (standard)
- Couples unfavorable reactions
- Drives biosynthesis
- Powers molecular motors

#### Other Energy Carriers
- **GTP:** Protein synthesis, signaling
- **NADH/FADH₂:** Electron carriers
- **Creatine phosphate:** Short-term energy buffer
- **Acetyl-CoA:** Carbon transfer

#### Cellular Energy Budget
- ~38 ATP per glucose (theoretical)
- ~30-32 ATP (actual)
- Energy efficiency ~40%
- Continuous ATP turnover

### 🔄 Cell Cycle and Division

#### G1 Phase (Gap 1)
- Cell growth and normal metabolism
- Decision point for division
- G1/S checkpoint (restriction point)
- Duration: 5-6 hours

#### S Phase (Synthesis)
- DNA replication occurs
- Chromosomes duplicated
- Histone synthesis
- Duration: 10-12 hours

#### G2 Phase (Gap 2)
- Preparation for mitosis
- Protein synthesis
- Organelle duplication
- G2/M checkpoint
- Duration: 3-4 hours

#### M Phase (Mitosis)
- Nuclear division
- **Prophase:** Chromatin condensation
- **Metaphase:** Chromosome alignment
- **Anaphase:** Sister chromatid separation
- **Telophase:** Nuclear envelope reformation
- **Cytokinesis:** Cell division
- Duration: 1-2 hours

#### G0 Phase
- Quiescent state
- Exit from cell cycle
- Differentiated cells
- Can re-enter under certain conditions

#### Cell Cycle Checkpoints
- Ensure proper progression
- DNA damage detection
- Prevent errors
- Tumor suppressors (p53, Rb)

### ☠️ Apoptosis and Cell Death

#### Intrinsic Pathway (Mitochondrial)
- Triggered by internal stress
- Cytochrome c release
- Apoptosome formation
- Bcl-2 family regulation

#### Extrinsic Pathway (Death Receptor)
- External death signals
- Fas/FasL, TNF receptors
- DISC formation
- Direct caspase activation

#### Caspase Cascade
- **Initiator caspases:** 8, 9, 10
- **Executioner caspases:** 3, 6, 7
- Proteolytic cascade
- Irreversible commitment

#### Regulation
- **Pro-apoptotic:** Bax, Bak, Bad
- **Anti-apoptotic:** Bcl-2, Bcl-xL
- IAPs inhibit caspases
- p53 tumor suppressor

#### Apoptotic Features
- Cell shrinkage
- Chromatin condensation
- DNA fragmentation
- Membrane blebbing
- Apoptotic bodies
- No inflammation

#### Necrosis vs Apoptosis
- **Necrosis:** Uncontrolled, inflammatory
- **Apoptosis:** Programmed, clean
- Different morphology
- Different triggers

### 🌱 Stem Cells and Differentiation

#### Stem Cell Types

1. **Totipotent:**
   - Can form entire organism
   - Zygote and early blastomeres
   - Highest potency

2. **Pluripotent:**
   - All cell types of body
   - Embryonic stem cells (ESCs)
   - Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)

3. **Multipotent:**
   - Limited cell lineages
   - Hematopoietic stem cells
   - Mesenchymal stem cells

4. **Unipotent:**
   - Single cell type
   - Tissue-specific progenitors
   - Limited self-renewal

#### Differentiation Signals

- **Growth factors:** TGF-β, FGF, BMP
- **Morphogens:** Gradients specify position
- **Notch signaling:** Cell-cell communication
- **Wnt pathway:** Stem cell maintenance
- **Retinoic acid:** Neural differentiation

#### Epigenetic Changes

- DNA methylation patterns change
- Histone modifications lock in identity
- Chromatin remodeling
- Progressive restriction of potential
- X-inactivation in females

#### Regenerative Medicine

- **iPSC technology:** Reprogramming somatic cells
- **Tissue engineering:** Organ regeneration
- **Cell therapy:** Replace damaged cells
- **Disease modeling:** Patient-specific models
- **Drug screening:** Test compounds on human cells

#### Challenges and Ethics
- Teratoma formation risk
- Immune rejection
- Ethical concerns (ESCs)
- Regulatory frameworks
- Long-term safety

## 🗄️ Hands-on: PDB Database Exploration

### Search Strategies
- **Keyword search:** Protein names, functions
- **Sequence search:** BLAST similarity
- **Structure search:** 3D similarity
- **Advanced filters:** Resolution, method, organism

### Structure Quality Assessment
- **Resolution:** <2Å excellent, >3Å lower quality
- **R-factor:** Crystallographic quality
- **Ramachandran plot:** Backbone geometry
- **Clash scores:** Steric conflicts
- **Missing residues:** Disordered regions

### Functional Analysis
- Active site identification
- Ligand binding sites
- Protein-protein interfaces
- Domain architecture
- Structural motifs

### Integration with AlphaFold
- AI-predicted structures
- Experimental validation
- Complement to PDB
- Structure coverage expansion
- Confidence scores (pLDDT)

- **RCSB PDB:** Main database (www.rcsb.org)

- **PDBj:** Japanese branch
- **AlphaFold DB:** Predicted structures
- **SwissModel:** Homology modeling
