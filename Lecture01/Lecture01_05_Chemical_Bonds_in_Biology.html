🔗 Hydrogen Bonding in Detail
⚡ Hydrogen Bond Formation
Hydrogen bonds form through electrostatic attraction between δ+ hydrogen and δ− oxygen. Although weaker than covalent bonds, they are the key factor determining water's unique properties.
❄️ Ice vs Liquid Water Structure
Ice forms a regular hexagonal lattice structure, resulting in lower density. In the liquid state, hydrogen bonds continuously form and break, creating a more compact structure.
📊 Water Phase Diagram

🔴 Triple Point

The unique temperature and pressure condition where all three phases (solid, liquid, gas) coexist in equilibrium.

🟠 Critical Point

Beyond this point, the distinction between liquid and gas disappears, forming a supercritical fluid.

🧬 Biological Importance of Water
🌡️

Temperature Regulation

High specific heat capacity helps regulate body temperature and protects organisms from rapid temperature changes.

🚚

Transport Medium

As the main component of blood and cellular fluids, it transports nutrients, oxygen, and waste products.

⚗️

Reaction Medium

Most biochemical reactions occur in aqueous environments, dissolving reactants for efficient interaction.

🔧

Structural Support

Maintains cell turgor pressure and provides structural support in plants.

🧫

Protein Folding

Drives the formation of three-dimensional protein structures through the hydrophobic effect.

🧪

pH Buffer

Acts as a weak acid-base to maintain stable intracellular pH levels.

🧂 Ion Dissolution Process (NaCl in Water)
The polarity of water molecules enables ionic crystal dissolution. δ− oxygen surrounds cations while δ+ hydrogen surrounds anions, forming hydration shells.
💦 Surface Tension & Capillary Action
🔵 Surface Tension
Surface water molecules experience attractive forces only from below and the sides, resulting in a net inward force. This creates a tendency for the surface to minimize its area.
🌱 Capillary Action
When adhesion is greater than cohesion, water rises up the tube. The narrower the tube, the higher the water rises (h₁ > h₂).
