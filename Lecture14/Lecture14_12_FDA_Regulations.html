Software as Medical Device (SaMD)
AI/ML-based Diagnostic & Therapeutic Tools
Definition & Scope

SaMD refers to software intended to be used for medical purposes that performs these purposes without being part of a hardware medical device. This includes AI and machine learning algorithms that diagnose diseases, guide treatment decisions, or monitor patient health. The FDA classifies SaMD based on the significance of the information provided and the state of healthcare situation.

Real-World Examples
  • IDx-DR: First FDA-approved autonomous AI diagnostic system for diabetic retinopathy detection
  • Caption Guidance: AI-powered ultrasound guidance system for cardiac imaging
  • Viz.ai: AI software for stroke detection in CT scans, enabling faster treatment
  • Eko Analysis: AI algorithm for detecting heart murmurs and AFib from digital stethoscope recordings
Key Considerations
  • Algorithm transparency and explainability requirements
  • Continuous learning vs. locked algorithms
  • Cybersecurity and data privacy compliance (HIPAA)
  • Real-world performance monitoring post-market

510(k) Premarket Notification
Substantial Equivalence Pathway
Process Overview

The 510(k) pathway is the most common route to FDA clearance, accounting for approximately 80% of medical device submissions. Manufacturers must demonstrate that their device is "substantially equivalent" to a legally marketed predicate device. This means the new device has the same intended use and similar technological characteristics, or if different, does not raise new questions of safety and effectiveness.

Device Examples
  • Digital Thermometers: Comparison to mercury thermometers showing equivalent accuracy
  • Blood Glucose Monitors: New models with improved user interface but same measurement technology
  • Pulse Oximeters: Wireless versions compared to wired predicate devices
  • Surgical Staplers: Ergonomic redesigns maintaining core functionality
Critical Requirements
  • Identify appropriate predicate device(s) still on the market
  • Average review time: 3-6 months for standard 510(k)
  • Special 510(k) for design modifications: 30-day review
  • Performance testing may be required for substantial differences

De Novo Classification
Novel, Low-to-Moderate Risk Devices
When to Use De Novo

The De Novo pathway is designed for novel medical devices with no predicate device available for a 510(k) submission, but for which general and special controls provide reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness. This pathway creates a new device classification and can serve as a predicate for future 510(k) submissions. It's particularly important for innovative technologies that don't fit into existing device categories.

Breakthrough De Novo Approvals
  • 23andMe Genetic Tests: First direct-to-consumer genetic health risk tests for conditions like Parkinson's disease
  • Apple Watch ECG: Over-the-counter electrocardiogram software for detecting atrial fibrillation
  • Parasight System: First AI-based malaria detection device using smartphone microscopy
  • BiliChek: Non-invasive bilirubin measurement device for newborns
Strategic Advantages
  • Creates new device classification for market exclusivity period
  • Your device becomes the predicate for future competitors
  • Review time: typically 6-10 months
  • Less burdensome than PMA while still novel

Premarket Approval (PMA)
High-Risk Class III Devices
PMA Requirements

PMA is the most stringent type of device marketing application required by FDA and is reserved for Class III devices that support or sustain human life, are of substantial importance in preventing health impairment, or present potential unreasonable risk of illness or injury. The PMA process requires extensive clinical data demonstrating that the device is safe and effective for its intended use. This typically involves multi-center randomized controlled trials.

PMA-Approved Devices
  • Heart Valves: Mechanical and bioprosthetic valves requiring extensive durability and safety data
  • Implantable Pacemakers & ICDs: Cardiac rhythm management devices with years of clinical follow-up
  • Ventricular Assist Devices: Life-sustaining heart pumps for heart failure patients
  • Cochlear Implants: Surgically implanted hearing devices with long-term efficacy studies
Important Factors
  • Average review time: 12-18 months after submission
  • Clinical trials often require 3-5 years before submission
  • Total development cost can exceed $50-100 million
  • PMA approval includes manufacturing facility inspection
  • Post-approval studies typically required

Breakthrough Device Designation
Expedited Development & Review Program
Program Benefits

The Breakthrough Devices Program provides manufacturers of certain medical devices with prioritized review and interactive communication with FDA experts throughout the development and review process. To qualify, a device must provide more effective treatment or diagnosis for life-threatening or irreversibly debilitating diseases or conditions, and must meet at least one of several criteria including representing breakthrough technology, having no approved alternatives, or offering significant advantages over existing alternatives.

Breakthrough Designations Granted
  • Watchman FLX: Left atrial appendage closure device for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation
  • MitraClip G4: Transcatheter mitral valve repair system for heart failure patients
  • Artificial Pancreas Systems: Automated insulin delivery systems for Type 1 diabetes management
  • Deep Brain Stimulators: Novel stimulation patterns for treatment-resistant depression
Qualification Criteria
  • Must treat life-threatening or irreversibly debilitating condition
  • Breakthrough technology or no alternative exists
  • Significant advantages over existing options
  • Device availability in patient's best interest
  • Can be applied with any regulatory pathway (510(k), De Novo, PMA)
