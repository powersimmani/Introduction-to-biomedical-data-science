Sanger Sequencing Recap
Method
Chain termination sequencing using dideoxynucleotides (ddNTPs)
Year Introduced
1977 by Frederick Sanger (Nobel Prize 1980)
Read Length
400-900 base pairs per read
Accuracy
99.9% accuracy (very high)
Key Characteristics
Gold standard for verification and validation
Low throughput - sequences one fragment at a time
Relatively expensive per base (~$500 per sample)
Takes several hours to complete
Best for targeted sequencing of specific genes
Clinical Use Today
Still widely used for confirming genetic variants and clinical diagnostics
How Sanger Sequencing Works
1
DNA Template Preparation
Purified DNA template is mixed with a primer, DNA polymerase, normal nucleotides (dNTPs), and a small amount of fluorescently labeled dideoxynucleotides (ddNTPs)
2
Chain Termination
During DNA synthesis, ddNTPs are randomly incorporated. These lack a 3'-OH group, stopping further extension and creating fragments of varying lengths
3
Capillary Electrophoresis
DNA fragments are separated by size through a thin capillary filled with polymer. Smaller fragments migrate faster than larger ones
4
Fluorescence Detection
As fragments pass the detector, a laser excites the fluorescent labels. Each base (A, T, G, C) emits a different color, revealing the sequence
Key Components
dNTP
Normal Nucleotides
Standard building blocks with 3'-OH group for chain extension
ddNTP
Dideoxynucleotides
Modified nucleotides lacking 3'-OH group, causing termination
🧬
DNA Polymerase
Enzyme that synthesizes new DNA strand from template
📍
Primer
Short DNA sequence that binds to template and initiates synthesis
Chromatogram Example
The output is a chromatogram showing peaks for each base. Peak height indicates signal strength, and peak position shows the base order.

Advantages vs. Limitations
✓ Advantages
Extremely high accuracy (99.9%)
Long read lengths (up to 900 bp)
Well-established and reliable
Simple data analysis
Gold standard for validation
✗ Limitations
Low throughput (one sample at a time)
Relatively expensive per base
Time-consuming process
Not suitable for whole genome sequencing
Requires high-quality DNA template
