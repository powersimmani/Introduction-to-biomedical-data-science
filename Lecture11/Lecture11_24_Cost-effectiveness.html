Detailed Analysis
1
QALY Analysis (Quality-Adjusted Life Years)
QALY is a measure that combines the quantity and quality of life gained from medical interventions. It represents one year of life in perfect health, with values ranging from 0 (death) to 1 (perfect health). This metric is crucial for comparing the cost-effectiveness of different treatments and making healthcare resource allocation decisions.

5.2 QALYs
Standard Care

Key Considerations:
  • Cost per QALY thresholds vary by country (e.g., $50,000-$150,000 in the US, £20,000-£30,000 in the UK)
  • Incorporates both life expectancy and health-related quality of life
  • Enables comparison across different diseases and interventions
  • Considers patient preferences and utility values

Budget Impact Analysis
Budget Impact Analysis (BIA) estimates the financial consequences of adopting a new healthcare intervention within a specific healthcare system. Unlike cost-effectiveness analysis, BIA focuses on the total expenditure impact over a defined time period, considering factors such as patient population size, treatment costs, and displacement of existing therapies.

  • Target population size and market share projections
  • Direct medical costs (drug acquisition, administration, monitoring)
  • Offset costs from reduced complications or hospitalizations
  • Time horizon typically 1-5 years
  • Sensitivity analysis for different adoption scenarios

Value Assessment Frameworks
Value frameworks provide structured approaches to evaluate the clinical and economic value of cancer treatments. Major oncology organizations have developed these tools to help clinicians, patients, and payers make informed decisions by scoring treatments across multiple dimensions including clinical benefit, toxicity, and cost.

Major Value Frameworks

ASCO Value Framework
American Society of Clinical Oncology framework evaluating clinical benefit, toxicity, and cost
ESMO-MCBS
European Society for Medical Oncology Magnitude of Clinical Benefit Scale
NCCN Evidence Blocks
National Comprehensive Cancer Network visual tool for efficacy, safety, quality, and affordability
Framework Applications:
  • Support shared decision-making between clinicians and patients
  • Inform formulary decisions and treatment pathways
  • Standardize value assessment across different therapies
  • Consider survival, quality of life, toxicity, and costs
  • Regularly updated based on new evidence and stakeholder feedback

Reimbursement and Payment Models
Reimbursement encompasses the processes and policies through which healthcare payers cover and pay for medical treatments. This includes regulatory approval, health technology assessment, pricing negotiations, and various payment models. Understanding reimbursement is critical for ensuring patient access to new therapies while maintaining healthcare system sustainability.

  • Risk-Sharing Agreements: Outcomes-based pricing with manufacturer
  • Managed Entry Agreements: Conditional coverage with data collection
Advanced Cost-Effectiveness Metrics
5
Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (ICER)
The ICER represents the additional cost per additional unit of health outcome gained when comparing a new intervention to the standard of care. It is calculated as the difference in costs divided by the difference in effectiveness between two treatments. ICER is the most widely used metric in health economic evaluations globally.

$28,000/QALY
ICER Interpretation Guidelines
The World Health Organization suggests interventions with an ICER less than 1× GDP per capita are highly cost-effective, 1-3× GDP per capita are cost-effective, and greater than 3× GDP per capita are not cost-effective. However, many countries have established their own specific thresholds based on their healthcare budgets and societal willingness to pay.

Healthcare Stakeholder Perspectives
6
Multi-Stakeholder Value Assessment
Cost-effectiveness analysis involves multiple stakeholders, each with different priorities and perspectives. Understanding these diverse viewpoints is essential for comprehensive value assessment and successful implementation of healthcare interventions.
👥
Patients & Families
Focus on quality of life, treatment efficacy, side effects, and out-of-pocket costs. Prioritize survival, functional outcomes, and access to innovative therapies.
⚕️
Healthcare Providers
Consider clinical effectiveness, safety profiles, ease of administration, and practice guidelines. Balance evidence-based medicine with patient preferences.
💼
Payers & Insurers
Evaluate budget impact, cost-effectiveness ratios, and long-term financial sustainability. Manage formularies and negotiate pricing agreements.
🏢
Pharmaceutical Companies
Focus on R&D investment recovery, market access, competitive positioning, and demonstrating value to justify pricing.
🏛️
Government & Regulators
Ensure public health, equitable access, healthcare system sustainability, and appropriate allocation of limited resources.
🎓
Health Economists & HTA Bodies
Conduct rigorous evaluations using standardized methodologies. Provide evidence-based recommendations for policy decisions.
⚠️ Key Challenges in Stakeholder Alignment
  • Conflicting priorities between immediate patient access and long-term system sustainability
  • Different valuation of outcomes (survival vs. quality of life vs. cost savings)
  • Varying willingness-to-pay thresholds across different healthcare systems
  • Balancing innovation incentives with affordability constraints
  • Addressing equity concerns in treatment access and distribution

Direct Medical Costs:

  • Impact on employment and career progression
  • Psychological and social costs to patients and families
Global Cost-Effectiveness Standards
9
International HTA Approaches
Different countries and regions have developed distinct approaches to health technology assessment and cost-effectiveness evaluation, reflecting varying healthcare systems, economic resources, and societal values. Understanding these differences is crucial for global market access strategies.
Country/Region HTA Body ICER Threshold Key Characteristics
United Kingdom NICE (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence) £20,000-£30,000/QALY Highly structured process, end-of-life considerations, societal perspective
United States ICER (Institute for Clinical and Economic Review) $50,000-$150,000/QALY Non-binding recommendations, multiple payer perspectives, value frameworks
Canada CADTH (Canadian Agency for Drugs and Technologies in Health) CAD $50,000/QALY Provincial decision-making, common drug review, pan-Canadian approach
Australia PBAC (Pharmaceutical Benefits Advisory Committee) AUD $45,000-$75,000/QALY Reference pricing, price-volume agreements, strict budget impact limits
Germany IQWiG / G-BA No explicit threshold Benefit assessment, reference pricing, mandatory manufacturer negotiations
🌍 Regional Variation Factors
Countries adjust cost-effectiveness thresholds based on GDP per capita, healthcare spending as percentage of GDP, disease burden, availability of alternative treatments, and ethical considerations such as severity of disease and unmet medical need. Low- and middle-income countries often use WHO benchmarks (1-3× GDP per capita) while high-income countries establish higher thresholds reflecting greater economic capacity and willingness to pay for health improvements.
Emerging Trends in Health Economics
10
Innovation in Value Assessment
The field of health economics and cost-effectiveness analysis continues to evolve with new methodologies, technologies, and policy approaches. These innovations aim to better capture value, improve access, and ensure sustainability of healthcare systems.
📱 Digital Health Integration
Incorporating data from wearable devices, mobile health apps, and remote patient monitoring to capture real-time outcomes and reduce monitoring costs. Enables continuous assessment of treatment effectiveness and early intervention for adverse events.
🧬 Precision Medicine Economics
Developing frameworks to evaluate companion diagnostics, biomarker-guided therapy selection, and personalized treatment strategies. Accounts for testing costs, treatment targeting, and improved outcomes in responsive subpopulations.
🤖 Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning
Using AI to predict treatment responses, optimize resource allocation, identify cost-saving opportunities, and automate health economic modeling. Enhances accuracy of budget impact projections and cost-effectiveness analyses.
💊 Novel Payment Models
Expanding outcomes-based contracts, indication-specific pricing, subscription models for curative therapies, and installment payments for high-cost treatments. Aligns payment with value delivery and manages budget impact.
🌐 Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA)
Moving beyond single ICER thresholds to incorporate multiple value dimensions including disease severity, unmet need, innovation level, and broader societal impact. Provides more comprehensive and transparent decision-making frameworks.
Future Considerations:
  • Integration of patient preference studies and discrete choice experiments into value assessment
  • Development of standardized real-world evidence generation protocols
  • Expansion of value-based care arrangements and risk-sharing agreements
  • Enhanced transparency in pricing negotiations and HTA decision-making
  • International collaboration on methodology harmonization and data sharing
  • Incorporation of environmental and sustainability factors into health economic evaluations
The future of cost-effectiveness analysis lies in comprehensive value frameworks that balance clinical efficacy, economic sustainability, patient-centered outcomes, and equitable access to drive optimal healthcare resource allocation
