Detailed Analysis
Ion Source - Ionization Process
The ion source is the critical first step where neutral molecules are converted into charged ions.

ESI (Electrospray Ionization): Applies high voltage (3-5 kV) to create fine charged droplets from a liquid sample. Ideal for proteins, peptides, and biomolecules. Produces multiple charge states: [M+2H]²⁺, [M+3H]³⁺, etc. This is a soft ionization method, preserving non-covalent interactions and molecular structure.

MALDI (Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption/Ionization): Sample is mixed with organic matrix compound and spotted on a probe. Pulsed UV laser (337 nm or 355 nm) desorbs both sample and matrix into gas phase. Matrix absorbs photon energy and transfers it to analyte molecules. Produces primarily singly-charged ions [M+H]⁺. Better suited for solid samples, complex samples, imaging applications, and tissue analysis.
Mass Analyzer Detailed Comparison
Type Operating Principle Resolution Advantages
TOF Ions accelerated and separated by flight time. Lighter ions travel faster through evacuated tube. High (>40k) Fast scanning, wide mass range, good for proteomics
Quadrupole 4 parallel rods create oscillating RF + DC electric field. Only specific m/z ratio passes through. Moderate (~1k) Robust, selective, ideal for SRM/MRM, triple quad
Orbitrap Ions orbit around a spindle-shaped central electrode. Orbital frequency proportional to m/z. Ultra-high (>240k) Highest resolution, exceptional mass accuracy <2 ppm
Ion Trap Ions trapped by AC + DC electric fields. Sequential m/z ejection for analysis. Moderate (~10k) MSⁿ capability, compact, versatile, sensitive
Understanding m/z (Mass-to-Charge Ratio)
The fundamental measurement in mass spectrometry. m/z = M / z, where M is the molecular mass (Da) and z is the charge state.

Example: Protein with M = 20,000 Da
• [M+H]⁺ (z=1): m/z = 20,001 ← Low sensitivity
• [M+2H]²⁺ (z=2): m/z = 10,001 ← Better sensitivity
• [M+3H]³⁺ (z=3): m/z = 6,667 ← Excellent sensitivity
→ Multiple charge states allow large proteins to be detected!
Tandem MS (MS/MS) - Fragment Analysis
Tandem MS involves two sequential mass analyzers separated by a collision cell. Essential for structure determination and sequencing.

MS/MS Workflow:
1. MS1: First analyzer isolates specific precursor ion (m/z value)
2. Collision Cell: Precursor collides with neutral gas (He, Ar, N₂) causing fragmentation
3. MS2: Second analyzer measures resulting fragment ions
4. Data: Fragment pattern reveals structure and composition
Peptide Fragmentation: b-ions (N-terminal fragments) and y-ions (C-terminal fragments) allow sequence determination via de novo sequencing.
Key Performance Metrics
1. Resolution: R = m / Δm. Ability to distinguish ions with similar m/z. Higher resolution separates isotopes and complex mixtures.

2. Mass Accuracy: Closeness to theoretical m/z. Formula: accuracy (ppm) = [(measured - theoretical) / theoretical] × 10⁶. Orbitrap: <2 ppm; Quad: ~10 ppm. Critical for elemental composition determination.

3. Dynamic Range: Ratio of largest to smallest detectable signal (10⁴-10⁶). Enables detection of trace and abundant analytes simultaneously.

4. Sensitivity: Minimum detectable amount (ng/μL, fmol). Depends on ionization efficiency, detector sensitivity, and ion transmission.
Practical Applications in Proteomics
Protein Identification: Enzymatic digestion → LC-MS/MS → database matching → identification

Quantification: Label-free, SILAC, TMT tags, or targeted SRM/MRM methods

PTM Discovery: Phosphorylation (+79.97 Da), Acetylation (+42.01 Da), Ubiquitination (+114.04 Da)

Biomarker Discovery: High-throughput identification of disease-specific protein signatures

Structural Biology: HDX-MS, cross-linking MS, native MS for protein dynamics and interactions
