P Phosphorylation Analysis
Phosphorylation Workflow
Overview
Phosphorylation is one of the most abundant and well-studied PTMs, playing crucial roles in cell signaling, protein regulation, and cellular processes. The addition of phosphate groups (PO₄³⁻) primarily occurs on serine, threonine, and tyrosine residues.
Key Technical Points
Target Residues: Serine (~86%), Threonine (~12%), Tyrosine (~2%)
Mass Shift: +79.966 Da (HPO₃) or +97.977 Da (H₃PO₄)
Enrichment: TiO₂ beads or IMAC (Fe³⁺/Ga³⁺) columns
Challenges: Low stoichiometry, neutral loss during fragmentation
Localization: Ascore, ptmRS, or MaxQuant site probability
Biological Significance: Regulates enzyme activity, protein-protein interactions, subcellular localization, and signal transduction cascades. Dysregulation is implicated in cancer, diabetes, and neurodegenerative diseases.
G Glycosylation Patterns
N-linked vs O-linked Glycosylation
Overview
Glycosylation involves the attachment of oligosaccharide chains to proteins, creating significant structural and functional diversity. It's one of the most complex PTMs due to heterogeneity in glycan composition and branching patterns.
Key Technical Points
N-glycosylation: Occurs at Asn in N-X-S/T sequons (X ≠ Pro)
O-glycosylation: Occurs at Ser/Thr, no strict consensus sequence
Core Structures: N: GlcNAc₂Man₃; O: GalNAc-Ser/Thr
Deglycosylation: PNGase F for N-linked, chemical β-elimination for O-linked
Detection: Mass shift analysis, glycopeptide enrichment (HILIC, lectin)
Biological Significance: Critical for protein folding, stability, cell-cell recognition, immune response, and cell signaling. Aberrant glycosylation is a hallmark of cancer and inflammatory diseases.
A/M Acetylation & Methylation
Lysine Modifications in Chromatin
Overview
Lysine acetylation and methylation are reversible PTMs that play fundamental roles in epigenetic regulation, particularly in chromatin structure and gene transcription. These modifications neutralize positive charges and alter protein-DNA interactions.
Key Technical Points
Acetylation: Adds acetyl group (COCH₃), mass +42.011 Da
Methylation: Adds methyl groups (CH₃), +14.016 Da per methyl
Target Sites: Primarily lysine, also arginine for methylation
Enrichment: Pan-acetyl-lysine or pan-methyl-lysine antibodies
Histone Code: H3K4me3, H3K9ac, H3K27me3, H4K16ac, etc.
Writers/Erasers: HATs/HDACs for acetylation, KMTs/KDMs for methylation
Biological Significance: Acetylation generally activates transcription by opening chromatin, while methylation effects depend on specific sites (activation or repression). Critical in cancer, aging, and metabolic diseases.
E PTM Enrichment Methods
Enrichment Strategy Comparison
Overview
PTM enrichment is critical because modified peptides are typically present at low stoichiometry (often <1% of total protein). Effective enrichment strategies can increase PTM detection by 10-1000 fold, enabling comprehensive PTM characterization.
Key Technical Points
Immunoprecipitation: Uses antibodies against specific PTMs (e.g., pan-acetyl-K, pTyr)
Affinity Chromatography: TiO₂/IMAC for phosphopeptides, lectin for glycopeptides
Chemical Methods: β-elimination for O-glycans, biotin tagging for click chemistry
Enrichment Factor: Typically 10-100× for IP, up to 1000× for IMAC/TiO₂
Considerations: Sample loss, bias toward abundant proteins, batch effects
Strategy Selection: Choose based on PTM type, sample amount, desired specificity, and downstream analysis. Often combine multiple methods for comprehensive coverage (e.g., IMAC + TiO₂ for phosphoproteomics).

G Section 2: Glycosylation Patterns - Translations
Diagram Components
Left Side - N-linked Glycosylation:
• Asn (N): Asparagine residue where glycan attaches
• N-X-S/T motif: Consensus sequence (N-any amino acid except proline-Serine or Threonine)
• GlcNAc: N-acetylglucosamine sugar residue
• Man: Mannose sugar residue
• The diagram shows the core structure with two GlcNAc residues followed by branched mannose residues

Right Side - O-linked Glycosylation:
• Ser/Thr (O): Serine or Threonine residues where glycan attaches via oxygen
• No consensus: Unlike N-linked, there's no strict sequence requirement
• GalNAc: N-acetylgalactosamine (first sugar attached)
• Gal: Galactose sugar residue
• Sia: Sialic acid (terminal sugar, often charged)

Analysis Strategies Box:
1. PNGase F digestion (N-linked removal): Enzyme that specifically cleaves N-linked glycans
2. β-elimination (O-linked release): Chemical method to remove O-linked glycans
3. Glycan structure analysis (HILIC, LC-MS): Hydrophilic Interaction Liquid Chromatography and mass spec to characterize glycan structures
Main Text Translation
Overview: Glycosylation involves the attachment of oligosaccharide chains (sugar chains) to proteins, creating significant structural and functional diversity. It's one of the most complex PTMs due to heterogeneity in glycan composition (different sugars) and branching patterns (how sugars connect).
Key Technical Points Explained
N-glycosylation: Occurs at Asn in N-X-S/T sequons (X ≠ Pro) - Asparagine followed by any amino acid except Proline, then Serine or Threonine
O-glycosylation: Occurs at Ser/Thr, no strict consensus sequence - More variable than N-linked
Core Structures: N: GlcNAc₂Man₃ (two GlcNAc, three mannose minimum); O: GalNAc-Ser/Thr (single GalNAc to start)
Deglycosylation: PNGase F for N-linked (enzymatic removal), chemical β-elimination for O-linked (base-catalyzed cleavage)
Detection: Mass shift analysis (glycans add significant mass), glycopeptide enrichment using HILIC (separates by hydrophilicity) or lectin (sugar-binding proteins)
Biological Significance: Critical for protein folding (helps proteins achieve correct 3D structure), stability (protects from degradation), cell-cell recognition (cell surface markers), immune response (antibody function), and cell signaling (receptor activation). Aberrant glycosylation is a hallmark of cancer (altered cell surface) and inflammatory diseases.
A/M Section 3: Acetylation & Methylation - Translations
Diagram Components
Histone Tail (N-terminal): The flexible N-terminal region of histone proteins that extends from the nucleosome core

Three States of Lysine:
• Unmodified (K with NH₃⁺): Lysine with positive charge, can bind negatively charged DNA tightly
• Acetylated (K-Ac, +42.011 Da): Acetyl group neutralizes positive charge, weakens DNA binding, opens chromatin for transcription
• Methylated (K-Me, +14.016 Da): Methyl groups don't neutralize charge but affect protein recognition

Methylation States Box:
• Me1 (Mono-methylation +14.016 Da): One methyl group added
• Me2 (Di-methylation +28.031 Da): Two methyl groups added
• Me3 (Tri-methylation +42.047 Da): Three methyl groups added (maximum for lysine)
Main Text Translation
Overview: Lysine acetylation and methylation are reversible PTMs that play fundamental roles in epigenetic regulation, particularly in chromatin structure (how DNA is packaged) and gene transcription (which genes are turned on). These modifications neutralize positive charges (acetylation) and alter protein-DNA interactions.
Key Technical Points Explained
Acetylation: Adds acetyl group (COCH₃), mass +42.011 Da - Removes positive charge from lysine
Methylation: Adds methyl groups (CH₃), +14.016 Da per methyl - Can have 1, 2, or 3 methyls on each lysine
Target Sites: Primarily lysine, also arginine for methylation - Both are positively charged amino acids
Enrichment: Pan-acetyl-lysine or pan-methyl-lysine antibodies - "Pan" means recognizes all acetylated or methylated lysines regardless of sequence
Histone Code: H3K4me3 (Histone 3, Lysine 4, tri-methylated), H3K9ac (Histone 3, Lysine 9, acetylated), etc. - Standardized nomenclature for specific modifications
Writers/Erasers: HATs (Histone Acetyltransferases) add acetyl groups, HDACs (Histone Deacetylases) remove them; KMTs (Lysine Methyltransferases) add methyls, KDMs (Lysine Demethylases) remove them
Biological Significance: Acetylation generally activates transcription by opening chromatin (making DNA accessible), while methylation effects depend on specific sites - some activate (H3K4me3) and some repress (H3K27me3) gene expression. Critical in cancer (aberrant silencing of tumor suppressors), aging (loss of epigenetic marks), and metabolic diseases (altered gene programs).
E Section 4: PTM Enrichment Methods - Translations
Diagram Components
1. Immunoprecipitation (IP) Box:
• Pan-specific antibody: Antibody that recognizes a PTM regardless of surrounding sequence
• Y-shaped structure: Represents antibody with binding sites for PTM-containing peptides
• ✓ High specificity: Can target exact PTM types
• ✗ Antibody dependent: Quality varies; expensive; may have bias

2. Affinity Chromatography Box:
• Column with beads: Shows packed column with TiO₂ (titanium dioxide) or IMAC (metal-containing) beads
• Green dots: Represent beads that bind phosphopeptides through metal-phosphate interactions
• ✓ High capacity: Can process large sample amounts
• ✓ Scalable: Works for small to large-scale experiments

3. Chemical Derivatization Box:
• PTM → Tag → PTM-B reaction: Shows chemical addition of biotin or other tag to PTM
• Examples listed:
  • β-elimination + Michael addition: Chemical reaction to tag O-glycans
  • Click chemistry for glycans: Copper-catalyzed addition of azide-alkyne tags
Main Text Translation
Overview: PTM enrichment is critical because modified peptides are typically present at low stoichiometry - this means that often less than 1% of a given protein is modified at any moment. Effective enrichment strategies can increase PTM detection by 10-1000 fold, enabling comprehensive PTM characterization that would otherwise be impossible.
Key Technical Points Explained
Immunoprecipitation: Uses antibodies against specific PTMs (e.g., pan-acetyl-K recognizes all acetylated lysines, pTyr recognizes phosphorylated tyrosines) - Antibodies bind and pull down modified peptides
Affinity Chromatography: TiO₂/IMAC for phosphopeptides (metal ions attract negative phosphate groups), lectin for glycopeptides (lectins are proteins that bind specific sugars)
Chemical Methods: β-elimination for O-glycans (base treatment releases glycan and creates reactive site), biotin tagging for click chemistry (adds a handle for enrichment using streptavidin beads)
Enrichment Factor: Typically 10-100× for IP (10-100 times more modified peptides than before), up to 1000× for IMAC/TiO₂ (especially effective for phosphopeptides)
Considerations: Sample loss (some material lost during washing), bias toward abundant proteins (easier to detect high-abundance proteins), batch effects (variability between experiments)
Strategy Selection: Choose based on PTM type (phosphorylation, glycosylation, etc.), sample amount (limited vs abundant), desired specificity (broad vs narrow), and downstream analysis needs. Often combine multiple methods for comprehensive coverage - for example, sequential IMAC followed by TiO₂ enrichment can capture different subsets of phosphopeptides for more complete phosphoproteome analysis.
